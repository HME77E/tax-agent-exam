NEBOSH GIC1 – Student Practice Exam

Independent NEBOSH-style training resource. These are original practice questions, not official NEBOSH examination questions. Each task carries 10 training marks. Answer in English and link points to the scenario.

Mock Paper 1: Construction Site – Tower Project

Scenario

A contractor is building a 12-storey residential tower. The programme is six weeks late. Subcontractors share access routes with delivery vehicles. A reversing truck narrowly misses a labourer. Several scaffold platforms lack toe boards, and workers say they have not received site induction. The supervisor tells workers to continue because the client is visiting. A worker reports a near miss but no record is made. The safety policy was signed three years ago, and management reviews focus only on cost and schedule.

Tasks

Task 1. Identify weaknesses in the organisation’s health and safety culture. [10 marks]
Task 2. Explain how management leadership contributed to the situation. [10 marks]
Task 3. Outline immediate actions following the vehicle near miss. [10 marks]
Task 4. Explain why near-miss reporting is important. [10 marks]
Task 5. Recommend ways to improve consultation with workers. [10 marks]
Task 6. Explain how contractor coordination could be improved. [10 marks]
Task 7. Recommend proactive and reactive performance indicators. [10 marks]
Task 8. Explain how an effective review and audit programme would help. [10 marks]

Mock Paper 2: Warehouse – Forklift Incident

Scenario

A distribution warehouse operates day and night. A new forklift operator was trained at a different site but has not been familiarised with this warehouse. Pedestrian walkways are faded. A pallet falls from a high rack after being struck. Temporary workers do not understand warning signs. The maintenance log contains repeated brake complaints. A manager says incidents are unavoidable in busy periods. No one was injured this time.

Tasks

Task 1. Identify the immediate and underlying causes of the incident. [10 marks]
Task 2. Explain shortcomings in competence management. [10 marks]
Task 3. Recommend how management should investigate this event. [10 marks]
Task 4. Explain the role of risk assessment in traffic management. [10 marks]
Task 5. Recommend suitable controls using the hierarchy of control. [10 marks]
Task 6. Discuss communication needs for temporary workers. [10 marks]
Task 7. Explain how maintenance failures should be managed. [10 marks]
Task 8. Identify records and evidence an investigator should obtain. [10 marks]

Mock Paper 3: Hotel – Fire and Emergency Preparedness

Scenario

A hotel is preparing for a large conference. Storage boxes obstruct a rear escape route. The fire alarm has generated false alarms and staff sometimes silence it without checking. A contractor uses hot work near combustible packaging without a permit. Several new staff do not know the assembly point. During a drill, guests enter the wrong stairwell and a person with reduced mobility receives no assistance.

Tasks

Task 1. Identify failures in emergency planning. [10 marks]
Task 2. Explain why emergency drills and debriefs are necessary. [10 marks]
Task 3. Recommend management arrangements for contractor hot work. [10 marks]
Task 4. Explain how vulnerable occupants should be considered. [10 marks]
Task 5. Discuss the significance of repeated false alarms. [10 marks]
Task 6. Recommend ways to communicate emergency arrangements. [10 marks]
Task 7. Explain how inspections could prevent blocked escape routes. [10 marks]
Task 8. Suggest management actions to monitor emergency readiness. [10 marks]

Mock Paper 4: Manufacturing – Chemical Exposure

Scenario

A small factory uses solvents to clean machine components. Workers complain of headaches and skin irritation. Safety data sheets are available only in the manager’s office. The local exhaust ventilation is noisy and has not been tested recently. Workers buy their own gloves. Production bonuses are based on output, and the occupational health provider has not been consulted. A worker reports symptoms but the supervisor suggests drinking water and returning to work.

Tasks

Task 1. Explain the employer’s moral, legal and financial reasons to act. [10 marks]
Task 2. Identify shortcomings in the risk assessment process. [10 marks]
Task 3. Recommend a suitable approach to controlling exposure. [10 marks]
Task 4. Explain the limitations of relying on PPE. [10 marks]
Task 5. Discuss the importance of health surveillance where appropriate. [10 marks]
Task 6. Explain how worker involvement could improve controls. [10 marks]
Task 7. Recommend an incident and ill-health reporting process. [10 marks]
Task 8. Explain how organisational incentives can influence behaviour. [10 marks]

Mock Paper 5: Facilities Management – Multiple Contractors

Scenario

A facilities company maintains a shopping centre. Electrical repairs, cleaning and roof maintenance are contracted out. Work permits are issued inconsistently. An electrician starts work without confirming isolation, and a cleaner slips on a wet floor where no barriers are present. Procurement chooses contractors on price alone. The client assumes contractors manage their own safety. A previous audit identified the same issues, but actions were never assigned to owners.

Tasks

Task 1. Explain the client’s responsibilities when managing contractors. [10 marks]
Task 2. Identify failures in the permit-to-work arrangements. [10 marks]
Task 3. Recommend a contractor selection and evaluation process. [10 marks]
Task 4. Explain how risk assessments and method statements should be coordinated. [10 marks]
Task 5. Discuss how audit findings should be tracked to closure. [10 marks]
Task 6. Explain how to prioritise corrective actions. [10 marks]
Task 7. Recommend ways to communicate between different contractors. [10 marks]
Task 8. Explain how management could verify that controls remain effective. [10 marks]

Mock Paper 6: Food Processing – Shift Work and Fatigue

Scenario

A food-processing plant has increased overtime after a major order. Employees work twelve-hour shifts for several consecutive days. Two operators report difficulty concentrating. A machine guard was removed to speed up cleaning. A minor hand injury occurred, but the shift leader recorded it as first aid only and did not investigate. Staff believe raising concerns may affect future overtime opportunities.

Tasks

Task 1. Identify organisational factors that may increase risk. [10 marks]
Task 2. Explain how fatigue can affect safety performance. [10 marks]
Task 3. Recommend a fair and effective reporting culture. [10 marks]
Task 4. Discuss immediate and underlying causes of the hand injury. [10 marks]
Task 5. Explain the importance of change management. [10 marks]
Task 6. Recommend arrangements for worker consultation. [10 marks]
Task 7. Suggest suitable monitoring indicators for management. [10 marks]
Task 8. Explain how lessons learned should be shared across shifts. [10 marks]
